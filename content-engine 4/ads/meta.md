# Platform: Meta Ads (Facebook + Instagram)

The lead service. Everything here assumes the objective is lead generation or conversions for local service businesses, the Kyndle default.

## The ad unit and where the hook lives

| Slot | Working limit | Hook rule |
|---|---|---|
| Primary text | ~125 characters visible before "See more" truncates in feed | The written hook lives INSIDE the first 125 characters, exactly like an organic caption. Everything after the fold is body copy for the already-interested. |
| Headline | Keep to ~40 characters, ideally under 27 so no placement clips it | The compressed claim: cover-text discipline, numbers beat adjectives |
| Description | ~27 characters, often not shown | Never put anything essential here; receipts or reassurance only |
| CTA button | Platform's fixed list | Match the ramp: Get Quote / Learn More / Sign Up; never Learn More when the page asks for a form fill |

Write 3 to 5 primary text variants and 5 headlines per ad; Meta's delivery system tests combinations, so every headline must work under every primary text.

## Formats

- **Static image:** 4:5 for feed (1080x1350, same canvas as the carousel system), 9:16 for stories/reels with the top and bottom ~14% kept clear of text (platform UI overlays live there).
- **Carousel ads:** the carousel format file applies with one change: every card must work standalone because Meta reorders cards by performance. No mid-thought endings; each card is its own hook + payoff. Card 1 still gets the Concept Engine treatment.
- **Video:** verbal + visual + written layers per the video format file; hook inside the first 3 seconds, designed for sound-off, captions always.
- The old 20% text-on-image rule is gone, but restraint still wins: one scaled keyword beats a paragraph on the creative.

## Compliance walls (the gate in GUIDE Step 5)

1. **Personal attributes.** Ads may not assert or imply things about the viewer: health conditions, financial status, and other personal characteristics. This kills a whole family of organic winners: "Your X is why you're broke" style second-person deficiency hooks. The standard reframes that keep the tension and pass review:
   - Second person to the problem itself: "Your website is costing you leads" becomes "Most contractor websites quietly leak leads."
   - Second person to first person: "I watched a contractor lose 40 calls to a broken form."
   - Second person to the audience noun: "For Dallas homeowners planning a fence" is fine; naming the audience is allowed, diagnosing the individual is not.
2. **Unrealistic results.** Specific outcome promises need substantiation; guarantees can run when they're real and the terms are reachable from the page. The "We Don't Stop Guarantee" is compliant because it's an actual service term; invented performance promises are not.
3. **Special Ad Categories.** Credit, employment, and housing offers trigger restricted targeting. The one that catches home services: FINANCING. An ad leading with financing terms can be flagged as a credit ad (relevant to any client offering financing, including Elevate). The play: sell the project, mention financing availability lightly, keep terms on the page; if financing IS the hook, declare the category and accept the targeting limits.
4. **Before/after caution.** Fine for fences and floors; restricted for personal health and appearance verticals. Know the client's vertical.
5. Policies shift and enforcement is uneven: when an angle sits near a wall, run the build-time policy check from GUIDE Step 2 instead of guessing.

## Meta-only research notes

- The Ad Library shows every active ad for any page: scan the client's top 3 competitors before writing. Ads running 60+ days are almost certainly profitable; log their angle, then find the gap.
- Retargeting copy can reference the relationship ("Still comparing fence quotes?" is product-aware and fine); cold copy cannot presume one.
