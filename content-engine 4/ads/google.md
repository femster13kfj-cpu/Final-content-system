# Platform: Google Ads

Search flips the hook problem. On Meta the ad interrupts someone who wasn't looking; on Google the person already searched, so attention is given and the job is MATCH plus DIFFERENTIATE. The written hook craft is identical, just aimed differently: signal is now "yes, this answers your exact query," and the scroll-stopper is whichever headline says it with the strongest receipt.

## Responsive Search Ads (the core unit)

- Up to 15 headlines at **30 characters** each, up to 4 descriptions at **90 characters** each. Google mixes and matches, so every headline must stand alone AND pair with any other. Pin only when a compliance or brand reason demands it; pinning fights the delivery system.
- 30 characters is the cover-text discipline squared. Same muscle, harder ceiling: numbers beat adjectives, one claim per headline, no wind-up words.

**The headline set covers five jobs (three-plus each for a full RSA):**
1. **Match:** mirror the search query's language ("Fence Installation Dallas"). This is signal; the searcher's own words are the strongest opener on this platform.
2. **Claim:** the differentiator, compressed ("Fences Built In One Week").
3. **Receipt:** proof from the active profile ("212 Five-Star Dallas Reviews", "10 Leads In 60 Days, Backed"). Receipts at 30 characters are where the profile's inventory earns its keep.
4. **Offer/guarantee:** the risk reversal ("Free Quotes. Financing Avail.").
5. **CTA:** the action ("Get Your Free Quote Today").

Descriptions carry one full Signal + Trust + Ramp sentence each; never four variations of the same sentence.

## Assets (formerly extensions): the receipt shelf

Sitelinks, callouts (25 characters), and structured snippets are free real estate that most local advertisers waste on filler. House the receipt inventory here: guarantee in a callout, services as structured snippets, the strongest proof pages as sitelinks. An RSA plus loaded assets can own twice the pixels of a lazy competitor's ad.

## Message match (graded, not suggested)

The searcher's query, the headline they clicked, and the landing page H1 must read as one continuous thought. Query "fence installation dallas" > headline "Fence Installation Dallas" > page H1 about fence installation in Dallas. Every hop that changes vocabulary leaks paid clicks; this is the message-match gate from GUIDE Step 5 with the query added as a third checkpoint.

## Policy walls

1. **Superlatives need substantiation:** "best," "#1," "top-rated" require third-party verification visible on the landing page. The compliant move is specificity instead: a real review count outsells "best" anyway.
2. **No gimmick formatting:** excessive caps, repeated punctuation, phone numbers or emoji in headlines get disapproved.
3. **Trademark rules:** competitor names in ad copy get flagged; competitor CAMPAIGNS target their keywords instead, with copy that differentiates without naming.
4. Same standing rule as Meta: near a wall, run the build-time policy check.

## Performance Max and Demand Gen notes

Both consume image and video assets: the visual layer supplies them per the ads GUIDE's two creative modes, sized per Meta's specs (the 4:5 and 9:16 canvases transfer directly). Copy assets follow RSA discipline. Treat asset group themes like ad sets: one awareness stage per group, never mixed.
