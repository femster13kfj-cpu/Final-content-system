# Format: Video

Short-form video first (Reels, TikTok, Shorts); long-form notes at the end. A video is the only format that uses ALL hook layers at once, which makes it the most powerful and the easiest to get wrong.

## The four hook slots

Every video has four hook slots, all pulled from the SAME goal section per the stacking rule:

1. **Spoken open** (verbal/patterns.md): the first words out of the mouth, or a deliberate silence per the Silent Decision below.
2. **First frame** (visual/patterns.md): what the eye sees at second zero. Doubles as the grid/feed thumbnail, so it's also the cover.
3. **On-screen text hook** (written/patterns.md via visual text family): the overlay line, six words or fewer, readable at thumbnail size.
4. **Caption** (written/patterns.md): its own separate written hook, never a transcript of the spoken open. Five confirmed dataset cases prove winners run different hooks in caption vs video, on purpose.

The alignment rule: slots 1-3 must bridge in the first sentence. The dataset's clearest failure (0.9x) had a strong text hook and a strong spoken analogy that never connected. Slot 4 may run a different hook, but the same goal.

## Assembly sequence

1. Goal (one of five) and topic.
2. Spoken open from the goal's verbal section, or Silent Decision.
3. First frame + text overlay from the goal's visual section, paired to the spoken open ([VERBAL] + [VISUAL]).
4. Script the body with verbal/structure.md: re-hook every 5 to 8 seconds, different type each time.
5. Close: resolve the loop, then the ramp (conversion/funnel.md). Mid-video keyword CTAs are verified for educational and leads content, placed right before the final step or at the halfway droop with a proof bridge.
6. Caption written separately from the goal's written section, plus the comment-keyword line if the ramp is a keyword.
7. Grade every slot against grading/rubric.md. Only A and B get filmed.

## The Silent Decision

Drop the spoken layer entirely when ALL of these are true (Silent Hook rules, 8 verified winners):
- The picture outruns any sentence: recognition, process, discovery, or aphorism content.
- The text overlay can carry the full written hook in one line or a two-line duality.
- Frame one contains a micro-action, not a static wait.
- Sound design (music, ambient, effects timed to movement) replaces the voice.
If silent, the text overlay is graded as the written hook, and the video is exempt from verbal checks but NOT from the ramp check.

## Runtime guidance by goal (from the dataset)

- Virality: shortest hooks, shortest videos. The biggest outliers ran 6 to 62 seconds; micro-hooks (1 to 5 words) dominate.
- Connection: silent pieces 10 to 16 seconds; spoken empathy pieces 50 to 110 seconds.
- Capturing leads: 28 to 60 seconds; every second past the payoff costs comments.
- Educational: 15 seconds (process) to 140 seconds (framework teaches); value density decides, not length.
- Authority: any length works IF re-hooked; verified winners from 6 seconds (silent card) to 230 seconds (coaching clips with section-level re-hooks).

## Video-only mechanics

- **First frame = thumbnail.** No logos, no intro cards, no mid-blink freeze. The frame must work as a static grid tile AND as second zero of playback.
- **Caption truncation:** Instagram shows roughly 125 characters before "more"; put the caption's hook entirely inside that window. TikTok captions truncate around 100 characters in feed.
- **Audio levels:** the first spoken word lands at full level; no music swell before the voice. For silent videos, the first sound effect syncs to the first movement.
- **Hashtags:** 3 to 5, after the caption hook, never before it.
- **The re-post rule** (150.2x verified): a flopped video likely wasn't distributed, not rejected. Delete, re-edit lightly, repost before writing off the concept.
- **Cross-post dedupe:** the same video on IG and YouTube Shorts is one piece of content in the swipe file, not two.

## Long-form note

Same four slots, same goal alignment. The skeleton scales: re-hooks stretch to every 20 to 40 seconds, chapters act as section-level re-hooks, and the ramp can appear twice (mid and end). The coaching-clip structure (someone else's stakes open, section-by-section escalation) is the verified long-form pattern in the dataset.

## Grading checks specific to video

Before filming, confirm: first three words graded as a mini-hook; frame one passes the static-tile test; slots 1-3 bridge in sentence one; a ramp is named (or its absence is a decision); runtime matches the goal guidance above. A video failing two or more checks goes back to assembly, not to the camera.
