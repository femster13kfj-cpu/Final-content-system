# Kyndle Slide Design System v1

Extracted from 29 slides across 4 complete carousels: three adarshxdesign sequences (Google AI tools, 9 slides, light interior; Adobe Firefly sponsored, 7 slides, light interior; Firefly workflow, 6 slides, dark interior) and one outside creator ("Claude sucks at writing content," 7 slides, print-editorial). Two creators in different visual worlds let us separate UNIVERSAL slide grammar (both do it = hygiene, adopt without question) from SIGNATURE (one does it = a choice to make deliberately).

Set 5 landed August 2026: @design.deb, "Clients don't hire the best designer," 10 slides. Third creator, third visual world. Its extraction lives in Part 6; anything all three creators do is treated as confirmed law.

**Profile note (Aug 2026):** every KYNDLE MAPPING below is really an ACTIVE-PROFILE mapping. Kyndle's values stay written out as the worked example; for client decks, substitute the palette, tint, and type pair from that client's file in `/clients/`.

## Part 1: Universal slide grammar (both creators, non-negotiable)

1. **One job, one proof.** Every slide carries exactly one idea and exactly ONE visual proof block: a screenshot, a diagram, a before/after, a chart. Evidence is embedded, never described.
2. **The number is a design object.** Progression labels (numbered badge, "SKILL 01" tab) appear top-of-slide, styled as artifacts. Numbers fuel completion: the viewer knows where they are and how much is left.
3. **Slide titles are mini-hooks and get the keyword treatment.** Both creators scale the slide's title word massively (tool names at ~90pt; "/DUMBIFY" full-width). The cover's keyword-scaling rule continues inside.
4. **Body text: 2 to 4 short lines, selective bolding of the payoff phrase, generous whitespace.** Neither creator ever runs a dense paragraph. If it needs more lines, it's two slides.
5. **Before/after carries a light/dark moral code.** Dark card or panel = the bad/old way; light card = the good/new way. Both creators use this exact coding (black "COMPLEX" card vs white "SIMPLE" card; dark before vs bright after with a red/green annotation circle). Adopt as law: in Kyndle slides, navy panel = the problem, light panel = the fix.
6. **The frame persists.** The header system (metadata strip or label+arrow) repeats on EVERY slide, same position, same size. The carousel reads as one document, not ten posters.
7. **Swipe cues are explicit.** An arrow top-right, chevrons, or an unfinished thought pointing rightward on every non-final slide.
8. **A tactile paper world unifies the deck.** Crumpled paper (adarsh) or print-proof stock with crop marks (creator 2). The analog texture makes embedded digital screenshots pop by contrast, and it brands the whole deck in one move.
9. **The CTA slide is a designed artifact, not an afterthought.** Creator 2 renders the keyword as the slide's typography plus a mock comment bubble showing someone typing it. Adarsh gives the CTA its own full photographic world plus his profile card and a "save for later" note. Both make the keyword itself HUGE. The action is illustrated, not requested.
10. **Density alternates.** Dense proof slides are relieved by lighter ones; adarsh literally alternates light and dark backgrounds mid-deck (slides 5 and 8 of the Google deck flip to dark) as a pacing pattern-interrupt, the carousel version of a re-hook.

## Part 2: The adarshxdesign interior signature (adopt into Kyndle)

- **The magazine strip on every slide:** [DATE] [HANDLE] [CATEGORY], persisting from the cover. On dark slides the handle flips to the accent color.
- **The serif-italic contrast word.** Titles pair a bold sans word with an elegant italic word ("Image *Generation*", "Generative *fill*", "as a *designer*"). The contrast is the elegance. KYNDLE MAPPING: the contrast word sets in Shadows Into Light, tying the slide system to our cover typography.
- **The capsule title frame with a topical tab:** slide titles sit inside a rounded-rectangle outline with a small tab attached carrying the topic's icon or label (Fi logo tab, "Fill" tab, "Video" tab). The tab is the slide-level version of the cover's UI prop.
- **Sticker and badge numerals:** black circle with white numeral on light slides, inverted on dark; the dark deck uses a peeling-sticker badge.
- **Pill tag rows as slide footers:** 2 or 3 rounded black pills naming use cases ("Research", "Content Generation"). Scannable taxonomy, and the pills invert on dark slides.
- **Embedded UI as the proof block:** actual prompt boxes with readable prompts, before/after image pairs with a circle annotation, sidebar screenshots with the relevant item highlighted in the accent color.
- **Two interior climates, one system.** Light climate: crumpled gray-white paper, black type, accent used sparingly. Dark climate: black smoky/chalk texture, white type, accent carries the titles and lead-in words. Same layout grammar in both. KYNDLE MAPPING: light climate = paper white with navy type and green accents; dark climate = navy #1A1A2E smoke with white type and green titles (display tint #2BD99F at title scale).
- **Sponsored variant:** hashtags replace the metadata strip's slots; the close slide swaps the comment-keyword CTA for a URL pill + device mockup. Useful the day Kyndle runs partner content.

## Part 3: Worth stealing from creator 2 (deliberate additions)

- **The slash-title device** ("/DUMBIFY", "/VIRAL-HOOKS"): names each item like a command. Perfect fit for AI-tools and systems content; reads as software.
- **The typography-only CTA slide:** the keyword in quotes at maximum size with a mock comment bubble mid-type. The highest-clarity CTA treatment in the collection.
- **Hand-drawn annotation arrows** over screenshots: pairs naturally with our Shadows Into Light handwriting and the cover squiggle.
- **The data chart as proof** (retention curves, good vs bad in two ink colors): our verified stats deserve this treatment.

## Part 4: The nine slide types, now with verified layouts

1. **Cover** — governed by the Cover Design System (editorial-surrealism.md).
2. **Slide 2 / second cover** — densest payoff; adarsh: number 1 + biggest tool + full prompt screenshot; creator 2: the strongest skill first with before/after. Never a table of contents.
3. **List/step slide** — number badge + keyword title + 2-4 lines + one proof block + pill tags.
4. **Comparison slide** — dark panel (bad) vs light panel (good), annotation circle or arrow between.
5. **Stat slide** — one number huge, chart in two colors max (creator 2's retention curve is the model).
6. **Framework/diagram slide** — the framework draws the eye path left to right (transcripts > DNA ribbon > voice file).
7. **Anti-example slide** — the failure rendered in the dark climate, the fix in light.
8. **Breather slide** — a single statement in the alternate climate; adarsh's dark flips ARE the breathers.
9. **CTA slide (second-to-last)** — keyword huge, action illustrated (comment bubble mock), profile card optional. **Receipt slide (last)** — everything compressed savable; can carry the cover's visual callback.

## Part 5: Production notes

- Slides are 4:5, built in the design template (hybrid path) for brand-exact Balboa + Shadows Into Light; generated imagery supplies scenes and textures, never slide typography.
- The crumpled-paper and navy-smoke textures get generated ONCE, approved, and reused deck after deck: they are brand assets, not per-post generations.
- Screenshots of real UIs stay real. Never generate fake interface evidence; the proof block's power is that it's true.
- Grade per the Slide Job Framework in formats/carousel.md: each slide against the metric its job serves.

## Part 6: Set 5 addendum (@design.deb, "Clients don't hire the best designer")

A five-point versus deck: 3D astronaut cover, one dark climate on a faint graph-paper texture from cover to close, every body slide the same layout. What it confirms, what it adds, and where it breaks our laws on purpose.

**Confirms, now 3 for 3 (laws hardened):** one job one proof; the number as a design object (angled "POINT 01" ribbon tab plus a stacked "PA/GE 01" monogram top-right, a new numeral styling worth having); the persistent frame (series label "LEARN DESIGN" top-left of every slide); explicit swipe cues on every non-final slide; embedded real screenshots as the proof block; the CTA as a designed artifact (an actual profile screenshot with the Follow button visible and a hand-drawn arrow pointing at it).

### New devices to adopt

1. **The red/green pill moral code.** design.deb never changes climate. Every comparison is coded by labeled pills sitting above the proof blocks: red pill = the bad side ("BAD PORTFOLIO", "₹500 logo", "Messy Canva"), green pill = the good side. This is a second moral-coding mechanism that works INSIDE a single climate, complementing law 5's navy-panel/light-panel code. KYNDLE MAPPING: green #1D9E75 pill = the fix, red pill = the problem. Red exists in Kyndle decks ONLY as the bad-side marker, never as an accent; green legitimately double-jobs as identity color and good side.
2. **The highlight-box keyword.** One payoff word per slide gets an accent highlighter box painted behind it ("because of THIS one thing", "Your BUSINESS skills are not"). It is the keyword-scaling rule rendered as an artifact, and it reads stronger than bolding. KYNDLE MAPPING: green box, one word max per slide, type color per climate.
3. **The versus-deck archetype (the one-layout deck).** All five body slides run the identical skeleton: point tab, claim title, one-line instruction subtitle, two labeled proof blocks side by side. Maximum consistency, minimum production cost, and the repetition itself becomes the rhythm. Use when the topic is a listicle of contrasts; the climate-alternating layout stays the default for mixed-type decks.
4. **The late thesis slide.** Slide 8 of 10 is a breather carrying the deck's entire argument in one sentence ("Your designs are already good enough. Your BUSINESS skills are not"), one icon, nothing else, placed just before the final transformation proof. The argument gets its receipt before the visuals do. Adopt as a breather variant: typography plus one line icon, thesis sentence with the highlight-box word.
5. **The save trigger names the moment.** "Save this before your next client call" beats "save for later" because it tells the viewer exactly WHEN they will reach for this again. Their close switches to an elegant script for that line; ours is Shadows Into Light. Rule added to formats/carousel.md.
6. **Infographic-as-proof for claims without screenshots.** When the proof is a relationship rather than an artifact, a flat one-glance infographic carries it: the seesaw with the ₹20K designer up and the ₹2L designer down inverts the price expectation in half a second. Same family as creator 2's data chart; the bar is one glance, two colors, zero explanation needed.

### Deliberate law-breaks observed (file as variants, not rule changes)

- **Slide 2 as receipt plus re-tease.** Their slide 2 delivers no payoff. It restates the cover claim with firsthand numbers ("I've seen ₹20,000/month designers beat ₹2 lakh/month designers"), proves it with the seesaw, and re-opens the loop ("Not because of skill. Because of THIS one thing"). For listicle decks this is a legal slide-2 variant: proof-of-claim plus mechanism tease, with density arriving at slide 3. Note the trust move: tier 5 (own observation) deployed in the first three seconds of the interior.
- **CTA on the LAST slide.** Their ramp is follow plus save, not a comment keyword, so there is no comment friction to protect and the final slide carries both actions. Our second-to-last rule stands for keyword CTAs; decks whose goal is authority or connection with a follow ramp may close on the CTA slide.
- **No climate alternation.** One dark climate throughout; pacing comes from the pill code, the infographic slides, and the late thesis breather instead. Single-climate decks are viable when the moral coding is pill-based; alternation remains the Kyndle default.

### Cover notes (observed, cover system unchanged)

The cover world (3D character on a moon, chains texture) stays theirs; editorial surrealism stays ours. Two transferable mechanics: headline words are color-coded by function (the negation word "DON'T" in red, the subject word "DESIGNER" in amber), and the headline runs partially BEHIND the character for depth, with the final cover line aiming straight at the swipe ("THEY HIRE THIS >"). For Kyndle covers the depth move is available as an occlusion option in generation: the figure or prop may overlap setup words for depth, NEVER the keyword. The color-coded negation word stays off-brand for us; our covers carry one accent only.
