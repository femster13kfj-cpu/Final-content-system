# Visual Hook Patterns

The visual hook is what the eye lands on before any word registers: the first frame of a video, the cover slide of a carousel, the graphic itself, the thumbnail. On mute, which is how most feeds are scrolled, the visual layer IS the hook.

This file applies to ALL formats. Format-specific mechanics (carousel slide rules, graphic sizing, thumbnail crops) live in formats/; those files pull from the goal sections here.

Evidence convention: video first-frame patterns below are verified against the July 2026 dataset (52 analyzed videos, outlier scores cited). Carousel and static-graphic guidance is principle-based until real carousels are graded against results, and is marked [principle].

## The eight visual hook families

**1. Camera angle and placement.** An unexpected vantage point: overhead process shots, POV, extreme close, low angle. The angle itself is the pattern interrupt.

**2. Movement.** Motion in frame one: walking toward camera, an action already mid-swing, anything that isn't a static talking head. The eye tracks movement before it reads anything.

**3. Transition and edit.** The cut as the hook: match cuts, whip transitions, before/after snaps. Works as a re-hook layer as much as an opener.

**4. Prop and object.** A physical object that signals the topic before audio lands. Props are silent semantic hooks.

**5. Action and micro-behavior.** The creator doing something slightly off or hyper-specific: pointing, a reaction in progress, a task mid-failure. Behavior that demands explanation.

**6. Reveal.** Something hidden then shown: opening, uncovering, flipping, the result held back until the eye commits.

**7. Text and graphic.** On-screen words as the visual: bold cover text, kinetic captions, a single keyword stamp. On mute, this family carries the entire verbal hook. Rubric visual checks govern: readable at thumbnail size, six words or fewer on covers, high contrast.

**8. Setting and production.** The environment as credibility and context. The setting makes a claim the creator never has to voice.

## The pairing rule

Every verbal beat gets a visual articulation: script each line as [VERBAL] + [VISUAL], naming which family carries the beat on mute. Deictic hooks ("this and this", "right over there on that ridge") are meaningless without the picture; they force total visual dependency and can only be planned with both layers together.

## Goal 1: Capturing Leads

**The Status-Matched Set** (setting)
Rule: the filming location must match the status level of the claim. Verified three ways: rooftop skyline for business authority advice (91.3x), lo-fi couch for insider platform tips (150.2x), car for hustle ideas (177.1x, 217.1x).
Watch-out: mismatch reads as fraud; a garage doesn't sell wealth advice, a penthouse doesn't sell scrappy tips.

**The Per-Item Overlay** (text)
Rule: in money listicles, each list item gets its own earnings/price overlay appearing the moment it's introduced (177.1x, 217.1x). Numbers on screen are the visual engine of leads content.
Watch-out: overlays must land in sync with speech; lagging text reads as sloppy.

**The Live Progress Badge** (text + graphic)
Rule: a dynamic counter or level meter that upgrades as the content progresses (view-count badge leveling 100 to 1M, 6.0x). Gamifies retention and visualizes the promise.
Watch-out: the meter must map to something real being demonstrated, not decoration.

**The UI Walkthrough** (prop + text)
Rule: for settings/tool tutorials, the interface IS the b-roll: greenscreen over the actual screens (823K views). The viewer follows along literally.
Watch-out: zoom to the exact tap target; full-screen UI at phone size is unreadable.

## Goal 2: Authority

**The Receipt On Screen** (text + prop)
Rule: the proof appears within 2 seconds of the claim being spoken: the actual LinkedIn post behind the story (94.2x). Say it and show it in the same breath.
Watch-out: a receipt described but not shown grades as a missing receipt.

**The Artifact Prop** (prop)
Rule: a physical object embodying your homework: handwritten notes tapped while teaching (23.0x). The artifact makes claimed depth visible.
Watch-out: the artifact must look genuinely used; a fresh clean page undercuts it.

**The Card Over Contrast** (text + setting)
Rule: a text-card thesis over b-roll that CONTRASTS it: asset-light empires over concrete being poured (649.4x, fully silent). The tension between text and image is the interest.
Watch-out: contrast is deliberate; loosely related b-roll just reads as random (Sandcastles flagged exactly this risk on the 649x video; it won anyway because the text was that strong, but don't count on that).

**The Word-for-Word Mirror** (text)
Rule: high-contrast text hook matching the spoken promise exactly, word for word (4.0x). Alignment across layers is itself a credibility signal.
Watch-out: paraphrased text under a spoken promise creates micro-distrust.

## Goal 3: Virality

**The Action Under Text** (action + text)
Rule: the attempt is already running while the fragments land: tattoo needle working under "No numbing cream" (51.4x).
Watch-out: never open on setup; open mid-attempt.

**The Embodied Metaphor** (setting)
Rule: the frame IS the metaphor the text speaks: real rain falling while the text talks about rain (435.9x, silent). Highest form of text-visual alignment.
Watch-out: shoot the moment when it happens; this pattern can't be faked in post.

**The One-Word World** (text + action)
Rule: a single word on screen ("Aura.") plus body language exaggerating that exact word (1,009.8x, silent). The acting is the definition.
Watch-out: the word must be culturally loaded enough to need no explanation.

**The Signal Prop** (prop)
Rule: one object in frame that telegraphs what's coming: a single cupcake on a plate means a reveal is imminent (7.8x). 
Watch-out: that same video was flagged for having NO text hook; a signal prop plus a text label would have graded higher. Stack the families.

## Goal 4: Connection

**The Raw Walk** (setting + movement)
Rule: outdoor walking selfie, zero production, plus a plain text card naming the audience (2,153.5x, the highest outlier in the pool). Rawness IS the credibility for empathy content.
Watch-out: polish kills it; a studio version of a pep talk reads as content, not care.

**The Chaos Under Simple Text** (action + text)
Rule: the chaotic NOW fills frame one while the text card states the simple past premise: kids climbing dad in the pool under "all he wanted was my number" (4,220x, silent).
Watch-out: the contrast does the work; calm footage under the same text is nothing.

**The False Peace** (transition)
Rule: a perfect, serene first 2 seconds engineered as bait before the disruption (476.7x night routine, silent). The setup makes the chaos land.
Watch-out: keep the peace SHORT; two seconds of calm, not ten.

**The POV Entry** (movement + angle)
Rule: the camera physically walks into the world as the mission is stated: entering the shelter (29.5x, 11% engagement). The viewer embarks, not observes.
Watch-out: pair with a spoken Mission Ask; entry footage without a mission is a location tour.

## Goal 5: Educational

**The Screen POV** (angle)
Rule: tutorial content opens inside the screen or process being taught (511.1x Gmail hack). The lesson starts before the explanation.
Watch-out: show the finished result (the moving icon in a real inbox) in the first 2 seconds; the crown-jewel video was itself flagged for skipping this.

**The Micro-Action First Frame** (action)
Rule: frame one contains a precise micro-action matching the text: needle entering the plushie under "Adding the final details" (337x, silent). 
Watch-out: process content thrives on the LAST 10% (finishing touches), not the whole build.

**The Live Diagram** (text + graphic)
Rule: the framework draws itself as it's taught: triangle appearing per step (65.9x), percentage table syncing to speech (2.1x).
Watch-out: sync is everything; the diagram completing early spoils the reveal, late feels broken.

**The Discovery Reveal Arc** (reveal + angle)
Rule: a fixed camera-move sequence: top-down establish, sweeping mid, extreme wide payoff (61.7x drone). The arc is the retention structure.
Watch-out: label text ("Most [superlative] [thing] in [place]") completes it; the arc without the label is just pretty footage.

**The Anti-Example Face** (action + transition)
Rule: perform the failure on camera before teaching the fix: acting out the bad intro, split-screen reaction to the fail (65.9x).
Watch-out: mock the behavior, never a real person's clip without their consent.

## Cover slides and static graphics [principle]

Until real carousels are graded against results, these rules govern covers and graphics; they derive from the text family, the rubric's visual checks, and the verified text-card winners above:
- The cover is a written hook wearing the text family: pick the line from written/patterns.md (same goal), six words or fewer, readable at thumbnail size.
- The Chaos Under Simple Text and Card Over Contrast patterns port directly to covers: text states one thing, the image tensions or embodies it.
- One focal element per cover. Numbers outperform adjectives on covers exactly as they do in written hooks.
- Kyndle house style (locked): navy/Oswald/green system per the carousel and lead-magnet skills; these patterns operate inside that style, they don't replace it.
