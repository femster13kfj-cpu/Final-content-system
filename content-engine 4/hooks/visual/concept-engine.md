# The Cover Concept Engine

How to generate a creative cover image AFTER the goal, topic, and written hook exist. The engine turns "we need a cover" into a forced sequence that reliably produces the kind of image where the topic's outcome is shown literally: a business physically sitting on top of Google for "ranked #1."

Used by: formats/carousel.md (cover slide) and formats/graphic.md (single images). Grounded in the dataset's top visual winners, which all run the same engine dynamically: the frame embodies what the words say (435.9x rain), or tensions against it (649.4x, 4,220x).

## The principle

Abstract claims are invisible; outcomes are drawable. Never illustrate the TOPIC (a laptop, a chart, a logo grid). Illustrate the OUTCOME of the topic, at its best or its worst, as a physical scene a stranger could describe in one sentence.

## The sequence

**Step 1: Write the two poles.**
One sentence each, concrete, no adjectives:
- BEST: what the world looks like when this topic fully works. ("Every customer searching sees this business first.")
- WORST: what it looks like when it fully fails. ("The business is buried on page five where no one has ever scrolled.")

**Step 2: Pick the pole by goal and pattern.**
- Capturing leads + Stakes Warning or Cost Reveal: WORST pole, or split frame.
- Authority + Before/After or Receipt Drop: split frame or BEST pole with the receipt visible.
- Educational + Gatekeeper, Tier Split, Time Collapse: BEST pole (the prize behind the gate).
- Virality + Myth Slayer or Open Question: ABSURD-LITERAL (Step 3, move 6) of either pole.
- Connection: usually NO metaphor. Real raw imagery wins this goal (2,153x sidewalk selfie, 4,220x actual family). The engine mostly stays off for connection covers.

**Step 3: Literalize with one of the six moves.**
Run the chosen pole through these until one clicks. Worked example throughout: topic = "ranked #1 on Google."

1. **Physicalize the Platform.** The platform becomes a physical structure. Google becomes a mountain, a podium, a building, a giant search bar. > A storefront perched on top of a massive Google search bar.
2. **Scale Play.** Shrink or enlarge for status. > The client's small shop towering over skyscraper-sized competitors clustered at the base.
3. **Personify the Business.** The business or owner becomes a character in the scene. > A business owner relaxing in a lawn chair on top of the Google "G," coffee in hand.
4. **The Trophy Room.** Status objects carry the rank: podiums, crowns, summit flags, spotlights, gold medals. > A gold #1 podium built out of a search results page, the business standing on it.
5. **The Split Frame.** Both poles at once, one image. > Left: a gray, dusty shop on "page 5." Right: the same shop glowing at the summit.
6. **Take the Idiom Literally.** Find the phrase people actually say and draw it with zero abstraction. "On top of Google" means ON TOP of Google. This is Karoni's canonical example and usually the strongest move because the language already did the creative work.

**Step 4: The one-glance test.**
- A stranger can describe the image in one sentence.
- ONE focal element. If the eye doesn't know where to land, cut elements until it does.
- Readable as a grid thumbnail: the joke or outcome survives at 150 pixels.

**Step 5: Pair the text, never repeat it.**
The cover text and the image split the job:
- Image shows the OUTCOME, text states the MECHANISM or promise. (Image: shop on top of Google. Text: "3 files put them there.")
- Or image shows the TENSION, text resolves it. (Image: buried page-5 shop. Text: "Invisible on Google? Fixable.")
Redundancy (image of Google + text saying "rank #1 on Google") wastes a slot and fails the grade.

**Step 6: Output three concepts, grade, pick one.**
Always generate two or three concepts from different moves, one line each, then grade against the rubric's visual checks plus the one-glance test. The first idea is usually the most generic; the pick is usually the second or third.

## Image generation prompt template

When producing the image with AI generation, structure the prompt as:
[The literalized scene in one sentence] + [single focal subject] + [pole mood: triumphant / bleak / split] + [style tokens: Kyndle navy and green palette, clean modern illustration or photoreal composite per asset type, high contrast, uncluttered background, 4:5]

Example: "A small barbershop building sitting confidently on top of a giant Google search bar, tiny competitor buildings far below, triumphant morning light, clean modern illustration, navy and green palette, single focal subject, uncluttered sky, 4:5."

## Guardrails

- Trademarked logos and platforms in generated imagery: stylize rather than reproduce (a search bar shape and colors that read as "Google" without being the exact logo). Keeps covers safe for ads later.
- The engine serves the goal sections; it never overrides the stacking rule.
- Worst-pole imagery needs a path out somewhere on the cover or slide 2; fear without a fix repels (rubric, Stakes Warning watch-out).
