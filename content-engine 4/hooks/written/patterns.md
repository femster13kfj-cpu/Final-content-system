# Written Hook Patterns

Named patterns for the first line of any written content: post openers, caption first lines, headlines, carousel cover text. Every pattern is niche-agnostic. Plug any industry, audience, or offer into the slots.

## The five goals

Every pattern lives under one of five content goals. This taxonomy is identical across the written, verbal, and visual folders.

1. **Capturing leads** — the reader should take an action (comment a keyword, download, book, DM)
2. **Authority** — the reader should trust this account knows what it's doing
3. **Virality** — maximum reach; the content should travel beyond the current audience
4. **Connection** — the reader should feel seen and closer to the person behind the account
5. **Educational** — the reader should learn something worth saving or sharing

**The stacking rule:** one piece of content serves one goal. When a format needs multiple hook layers (a video needs verbal + visual + written), every layer pulls from the same goal section in its folder. Mismatched goals across layers cancel each other out.

Patterns that genuinely serve a second goal carry an "Also serves" tag. Fill-in-the-blank variants live in templates.md under the same names. Proven uses live in examples.md.

Sources: distilled from the PBL 1000 Viral Hooks library, Sandcastles outlier analysis (July 2026), and the grading rubric. The full 1,044-hook source library lives in archive/ for variety mining.

**Source category map.** The source library's 7 categories fold into the five goals like this: Educational → Educational; Storytelling → Connection; Myth Busting → Virality; Authority → Authority; Comparison → Educational (The Tier Split); Day in the Life → Connection (The Role Peek); Random → Virality (pure pattern interrupts). Nothing from the source is orphaned; every category has a home in the goal system.

## Goal 1: Capturing leads

**The Direct Call-Out**
Engine: audience match. The target person is named so precisely they cannot scroll past.
Skeleton: If you're a [audience] and you want [dream result], [payoff or instruction].
When: audience-specific advice, offers, lead magnets. The workhorse pattern for service businesses.
Watch-out: the narrower the call-out, the harder it stops. "Business owners" is weak. "Lansing HVAC owners running ads with no calls" is a wall.

**The Receipt Drop**
Engine: specificity + believability. A hard number up front does the stopping.
Skeleton: [Specific number or result], and here's exactly how.
When: results content, cost breakdowns, income or savings proof.
Also serves: authority.
Watch-out: the number must be provable in the content or it becomes an automatic fail.

**The Stakes Warning**
Engine: loss aversion. Names the cost of inaction.
Skeleton: If you don't [action] you'll end up [specific pain] by [time]. / Stop [common action] if you actually want [result].
When: urgency-driven advice, prevention topics, pre-offer education.
Watch-out: fear without a path forward repels. Always carry the fix inside the content.

**The Cost Reveal**
Engine: specificity + taboo. Money numbers people usually hide, shown in public.
Skeleton: This is what [$ amount] gets you in [context]. / Here's the full cost breakdown of [thing].
When: pricing transparency, budget content, value demonstrations.
Also serves: educational.
Watch-out: round suspicious numbers read fake. $4,850 outperforms $5,000.

## Goal 2: Authority

**The Before/After**
Engine: specificity + curiosity. The gap between two states begs the question "how."
Skeleton: I went from [bad state] to [good state] in [timeframe].
When: transformation proof, case studies, client results.
Also serves: capturing leads.
Watch-out: both states need concrete detail. "Invisible on Google" to "page one" beats "struggling" to "thriving."

**The Credential Flex**
Engine: believability. Authority stated up front so every following word carries weight.
Skeleton: I'm a [title] who [specific proof]. Here's [what that taught me / what I'd do].
When: expert positioning, "if I were starting over" content, advice from experience.
Watch-out: the credential must be specific and earned. "As an expert" is not a credential.

**The Experiment**
Engine: curiosity gap with built-in payoff structure. A test was run; results are inside.
Skeleton: I tried [action] for [timeframe]. Here's what happened.
When: challenges, tool tests, method reviews, series content.
Also serves: educational.
Watch-out: the experiment parameters need a number. "For a while" kills it.

## Goal 3: Virality

**The News-Jack**
Engine: scroll-stop through urgency. Something just changed and the reader is behind.
Skeleton: [Authority] just [killed / changed / announced] [thing the audience relies on].
When: platform updates, industry changes, algorithm shifts. Among the highest outliers in the July 2026 data (up to 400x).
Watch-out: the change must be real and recent. Manufacturing urgency burns trust permanently.

**The Myth Slayer**
Engine: scroll-stop through contradiction. Attacks a belief the audience holds.
Skeleton: Everyone tells you [common advice]. [It's wrong / nobody shows you how]. Here's [the truth / the how].
When: repositioning against competitors, correcting industry misconceptions.
Also serves: authority.
Watch-out: needs a receipt. Contrarian without proof reads as engagement bait.

**The Open Question**
Engine: curiosity gap in interrogative form. The brain cannot leave a direct question unanswered.
Skeleton: What if I told you [surprising claim]? / What happens when you [extreme or unusual action]?
When: intros to surprising info, scenario content, top-of-funnel education.
Watch-out: the question must not be answerable with "nothing" or "who cares." Stakes belong inside the question.

## Goal 4: Connection

**The Mirror**
Engine: instant recognition. The reader sees their exact situation and stops out of "that's literally me."
Skeleton: POV: [hyper-specific lived situation]. / You know that feeling when [precise shared moment].
When: relatability content, humanizing a brand.
Also serves: virality (recognition content travels).
Watch-out: almost no curiosity loop needed, but the situation must be precise. Generic relatability stops no one.

**The Story Drop**
Engine: curiosity gap through in-progress narrative. Opens mid-story with a detail that demands context.
Skeleton: [Timeframe] ago I [surprising decision or event]. / I don't have a backup plan, so this kind of needs to work.
When: personal brand content, founder journeys, milestone posts.
Watch-out: the opening line must contain the tension, not the setup. Start at the interesting part.

**The Role Peek**
Engine: aspirational voyeurism. Access to a day inside a life or job the viewer is curious about.
Skeleton: Day in the life of a [identity with a twist]. / Come to work with me as a [title].
When: humanizing a brand, behind-the-scenes content, recruiting, founder journey.
Also serves: authority (competence shown in passing reads stronger than competence claimed).
Watch-out: a generic routine is wallpaper. The identity needs a twist, a stake, or a countdown attached.

**The Series Loop**
Engine: appointment viewing. A running counter turns one post into an ongoing story the viewer joins mid-stream and returns for.
Skeleton: Day [N] of [pursuit] until [dream result]. / Welcome back to [series name], where I [premise].
When: challenges, builds-in-public, transformations, documented client journeys.
Also serves: capturing leads (returning viewers become followers become leads).
Watch-out: every episode must still stand alone. The counter rewards regulars; the content must work for first-timers.

## Goal 5: Educational

**The Gatekeeper Open**
Engine: curiosity gap. Promises access to something hidden or hoarded.
Skeleton: I found [the cheat code / the thing nobody shares] for [result]. / I've been gatekeeping [thing], today I'm giving it away.
When: tips, tools, insider knowledge, "save this" content.
Also serves: capturing leads (natural pairing with comment-keyword CTAs).
Watch-out: the reveal must actually feel insider-level, or the comment section will say so.

**The Time Collapse**
Engine: value density. Years of learning compressed into seconds of consumption.
Skeleton: It took me [long time] to learn this. I'll teach it to you in [tiny time].
When: educational content, quick tutorials, authority building.
Also serves: authority.
Watch-out: the content must actually deliver the compression. A 3-minute ramble breaks the promise.

**The Tier Split**
Engine: clarity + curiosity. A ranked comparison the reader must see to place themselves.
Skeleton: Bad [thing] vs good [thing] vs great [thing]. / Cheap vs expensive [thing].
When: teaching quality standards, product comparisons, portfolio content.
Watch-out: the tiers must be visibly, obviously different or the comparison collapses.

## Using this file

1. Name the goal of the content first. One goal per piece.
2. Choose patterns only from that goal's section (or a pattern that "Also serves" it).
3. Pull templates from templates.md under the same pattern names.
4. Grade against the rubric. The pattern gets you to the table; the rubric decides if you eat.
