# The Conversion Layer

A hook that stops the scroll but converts nothing is entertainment. This file governs what happens to attention after it's captured. It applies to every piece of content in every format, alongside whichever goal section the hooks came from.

## The multiplication rule

Conversion is Signal x Trust x Ramp x Offer. This is multiplication, not addition: if any one factor is a zero, the result is zero. This is why accounts with millions of views can sell nothing while small accounts print money. When content gets attention but produces no leads, find the factor that's a zero and fix that one.

## Factor 1: Signal

The first one or two lines must give the right viewer total certainty that this is a potential solution to their specific pain or desire. The brain sorts every post into three buckets: potential solution (the only bucket that makes money), entertainment (watched, enjoyed, forgotten), or irrelevant (the default when confused). Confusion is the number one conversion killer.

Five levers that sharpen signal: extreme topic specificity, a crystal-clear hook, naming the pain emotion directly, a before/after transformation, and branding your method with a name.

## Factor 2: Trust, in weighted tiers

Every credibility signal deposits trust. Receipts are not equal; they tier from weakest to strongest:

| Tier | Receipt | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Stats, studies, third-party data | Weakest. Forces the viewer to wonder "were they like me?" |
| 7 | One testimonial from an unknown person | Weak. Easily faked. |
| 6 | Borrowed authority (a respected figure's framework, named) | Low-mid |
| 5 | Your own result, applied to yourself | Mid |
| 4 | A named pattern of client results (multiple, specific) | High |
| 3 | Live demonstration of ability inside the content itself | High. Can't be faked. |
| 2 | Visible audience size | Very high. Viewers outsource trust to the crowd. |
| 1 | A warm referral from someone the viewer already trusts | Strongest. Supersedes everything. This is why share loops matter. |

Stack two or three tiers in one piece rather than leaning on one. The higher the viewer's pain, the fewer receipts they need; low-pain audiences need a large trust balance before they act.

Guardrail: fresh framing of an old idea is craft; claiming old news as new discovery is overclaiming. The rubric's believability criterion governs. When in doubt, the receipt decides.

## Factor 3: The Ramp

Captured, trusting attention with no off-ramp just keeps scrolling. Every piece of content names its ramp before it ships. Three types:

- Lead magnet (free resource for a contact): lowest friction, the default, builds the email/DM asset, keeps depositing trust
- Sales page or direct offer: for low-ticket, or high-ticket when trust is very large
- Call or DM conversation: for high-ticket that needs handholding

Alignment rule: the ramp must match the content's topic. A post about X with an off-ramp to Y converts nobody.

## Factor 4: The Offer

A purchase is a risk calculation: "if I pay, how likely am I to get the promised result?" Price raises risk; trust lowers it. De-risk with a guarantee or by engineering the offer so following it plausibly returns a multiple of its cost. The offer must solve the exact pain the hook teased, or the whole chain breaks at the last link.

## The ramp check (mandatory)

Before any content ships, answer in one line: what is the ramp, and does it align with the topic? Content with no ramp gets flagged the same way a C-grade hook does. It can still ship (pure connection or virality plays sometimes run rampless on purpose), but the omission must be a decision, never an oversight.
