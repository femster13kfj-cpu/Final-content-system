# Verbal Hook Patterns

The verbal hook is the first spoken seconds of a video: the words that come out of your mouth before the viewer decides to stay. This file covers Layer 1 (the primary hook, 0 to 3 seconds); structure.md governs everything after second three.

Organized by the same five goals as written/ and visual/. The stacking rule applies: one piece of content, one goal, every layer pulls from that goal's section. The caption is a SEPARATE written-hook slot (see Conventions below).

Evidence convention: every pattern cites the verified winners behind it with outlier scores from the July 2026 dataset (52 analyzed videos). Patterns marked [delivery model] come from lower-outlier teaching specimens and carry style weight, not performance proof.

---

## The Silent Hook (cross-goal, read first)

Sometimes the strongest verbal hook is no words at all. EIGHT winners in the dataset have zero spoken opening, spanning four goals: the aura skit (1,009.8x), the family pool video (4,220x), the night routine (476.7x), the rain aphorism (435.9x), the Uber text card (649.4x), the crochet close-up (337x), the drone reveal (61.7x), and the mirror-symmetry run (31.6x).

Choose silence when the picture outruns any sentence: recognition moments, physical processes, discoveries, and aphorisms. The rules:
1. The visual must EMBODY the text. Rain on screen while the text talks about rain. Chaos in frame while the text says "now this is his life."
2. Text stays minimal: one word ("Aura."), a label ("Most beautiful cricket playground in India"), or a two-line duality.
3. Frame one contains a micro-action (needle entering fabric, kids mid-leap), never a static wait.
4. Sound design replaces speech: lullaby, ambient rain, sound effects timed to movement.

A silent hook still gets graded: the on-screen text is scored as a written hook, and the first frame as a visual hook.

## Goal 1: Capturing Leads

**The Command Open**
Skeleton: [Imperative instruction], stated as the very first words. Optionally repeated verbatim.
Evidence: "If you have any type of business, buy a domain name..." (91.3x); "Delete that video and post it again. Delete that video and post it again." (150.2x); "Never post on Instagram again without turning on these five settings." (823K views).
Delivery: firm, zero wind-up; the instruction IS the hook. Repetition reads as urgency.
Watch-out: the commanded action must be cheap and immediate, or it reads as a sales pitch.

**The Money-Metric List**
Skeleton: [Number] [things] that will [money result], and how much each can make you.
Evidence: haydengarlock wedding ideas (177.1x) and boring businesses (217.1x); identical skeleton, one variable changed, both monsters. Series-repeatable.
Delivery: promise the per-item numbers in the hook itself, then pay one number per item.
Watch-out: numbers must be concrete and plausible per item; one personal receipt on ONE item ("my wife did this one") reads authentic.

**The Library Tease**
Skeleton: These [resources] [win] time and time again. / [Category label] + title card.
Evidence: viral songs library (15,911 comments); Sound Effects 101 (42.6x, 22K comments); 4 YouTube videos (14.5x, 4,924 comments). The highest comments-per-view formats in the dataset.
Delivery: the hook is modest because the value density is the hook; tier the examples by results; hold the full library behind a comment keyword.
Watch-out: the free samples shown must be genuinely good, or the keyword comment never comes.

**The Stakes Gate**
Skeleton: You will never [result] if you don't [skill/action].
Evidence: devinjatho speak-on-camera loop (6.0x); the negative-constraint cousin of the Command Open.
Delivery: pairs with a demonstration payoff; the video must prove the gate is real.
Watch-out: gate on a skill you can actually teach in the video, never on buying something.

## Goal 2: Authority

**The Proxy Open**
Skeleton: [Someone else's bold claim or stakes], told as a story. / The guest speaks first.
Evidence: "This guy wrote on LinkedIn, 'I'm the world's most renowned AI visibility expert'" (94.2x); hormozi Q&A clips where the student's revenue claim opens ("I sell multi-million dollar houses..." 3.6x, "$1.2M to $3M" 5.2x).
Delivery: your authority arrives sideways, by decoding or coaching someone else. Receipt on screen within 2 seconds of the claim.
Watch-out: the anomaly or stakes must be real and shown, not narrated.

**The Compression Promise**
Skeleton: I'm gonna compress [long period] of [niche] advice into this one video.
Evidence: hormozi 13 years (4.0x); the spoken form of written's Time Collapse.
Delivery: then actually compress: every sentence a standalone directive, no connective tissue.
Watch-out: one rambling section breaks the promise and the grade.

**The Insider Percentile**
Skeleton: If you [consume this], you'll know more than [99%] of [group].
Evidence: dante Meta deep-dive (23.0x), claim anchored to a physical prop (handwritten notes he taps while teaching).
Delivery: pair the percentile claim with a visible artifact of the depth being promised.
Watch-out: the content afterward must be genuinely deeper than the niche's normal fare.

**The Parallel Fact Stack** (silent-capable)
Skeleton: [Entity] owns no [asset]. [Entity] owns no [asset]. > [thesis line].
Evidence: joserbuilds (649.4x), fully silent text card.
Delivery: three to five parallel facts, identical grammar, then one takeaway. Works spoken or as pure text.
Watch-out: every fact must be instantly verifiable common knowledge; one shaky item breaks the spell.

## Goal 3: Virality

**The Micro Binary**
Skeleton: [Option A] or [option B]? (five words or fewer)
Evidence: "Big house or little house?" (69.2x), followed instantly by a vivid scene.
Delivery: the question forces an internal pick before the viewer decides to stay; follow with a scenario, not an answer.
Watch-out: both options must be lives people actually claim; a strawman option kills it.

**The Spoken News-Jack**
Skeleton: [Authority] just [killed/changed] [thing you rely on]. / Most [audience] will notice [symptom] before they understand why.
Evidence: neilpatel 400.6x and 260x, the two highest niche outliers in the free dataset.
Delivery: urgency in the voice, receipt (the actual announcement) promised immediately.
Watch-out: manufactured urgency is a rubric automatic fail; the change must be real and recent.

**The Fragment Stack**
Skeleton: [Task]. [Duration]. [Constraint]. Telegraph style, three fragments.
Evidence: "Challenge: the most painful tattoo spot—armpit. Two days in a row. No numbing cream." (51.4x).
Delivery: each fragment is its own mini-hook; action starts under the words.
Watch-out: three fragments maximum; a fourth turns it into a paragraph.

**The Rule-Setting Game**
Skeleton: Okay, here's what we're gonna do. We're gonna [action], but [constraint].
Evidence: hormozi blind gender reveal (7.8x); the constraint manufactures the tension.
Delivery: state the rules like a game; the struggle against the constraint is the content.
Watch-out: the constraint must make the outcome genuinely uncertain on camera.

## Goal 4: Connection

**The Identity Gate**
Skeleton: If you're actual [hyper-specific identity], like [definitive criterion]...
Evidence: chuckbennington (2,153.5x, the highest outlier in the entire pool): "If you're actual big boy self-employed, like 100% of your household income is derived from your business."
Delivery: spend real seconds on the gate (his runs 18); exclusion is the point. Then name the UNSPOKEN burden the insiders carry, then reframe it as victory.
Watch-out: you must belong to, or deeply serve, the identity you're gating; outsiders performing insider talk get destroyed in comments.

**The Expectation Deflator**
Skeleton: I hate when people [common question] like I'm out here [exaggerated expectation] or something.
Evidence: midlife weekend (449.3x): the whole arc runs 5 seconds of speech.
Delivery: deadpan; then one absurdly specific real detail (shredded cheese from the bag) that converts relatable into viral.
Watch-out: the specific detail does all the work; generic "I stayed home" stops no one.

**The Mundane Cold Open**
Skeleton: [Ordinary line of real dialogue], mid-scene.
Evidence: "Hey can you pass me my sweater? It's right behind you." (62.0x, 59M views).
Delivery: the tinier the request, the funnier the elaboration; comedy lives in the delay, not the line.
Watch-out: needs the skit to escalate visually; the line alone is nothing.

**The Role Peek Intro**
Skeleton: Welcome to a day in the life of a [age]-year-old [identity] who [humble task] for a living.
Evidence: coffee trailer Sunday (49.0x); a verbal tic repeated through the montage ("my friend" ~20 times) builds familiarity.
Delivery: age + identity + humble concrete task; adopt one repeatable signature phrase.
Watch-out: needs a twist, stake, or rhythm; a flat routine is wallpaper.

**The Mission Ask**
Skeleton: [Request to a third party on camera]: Can we [kind action] for [someone overlooked]?
Evidence: Mama Moo shelter day (29.5x, 11% engagement, highest in pool): mission > backstory > joyful payoff stream > promise.
Delivery: the ask is spoken to someone in-scene, not to the audience; the viewer overhears a mission starting.
Watch-out: the backstory beat is the stakes; skip it and the montage is just b-roll.

**The Soft Promise** [delivery model]
Skeleton: I hope this story can change your life. + deictic grounding ("right over there on that ridge").
Evidence: dante Bryan Johnson story (44.1x); closes with explicit permission-giving ("This is me giving you permission to take the shot").
Delivery: humble promise in, emotional permission out; pointing at real places makes the story physical.
Watch-out: earn the promise with real stakes inside; deictic lines require the camera to show the place.

## Goal 5: Educational

**The Stat Reminder**
Skeleton: Just a reminder that [big stat], and this is the easiest [hack] that anybody can do.
Evidence: Gmail logo hack (511.1x, the niche crown jewel). Closes by repeating the stat bigger (22% global > 75% US).
Delivery: "just a reminder" dresses a shocking stat as common knowledge; close the loop by reprising the number.
Watch-out: the stat must be checkable; the promised ease must be real (his was genuinely 4 steps).

**The Diagnosis**
Skeleton: The reason you can't [result] is because you don't have a [named thing] for it.
Evidence: Triangle of Yapping (65.9x, 13K comments); includes the anti-example (performing the failure) as a built-in secondary hook.
Delivery: diagnose, act out the failure, then reveal the named cure; drop the lead magnet right before the final step.
Watch-out: the framework must actually resolve the diagnosis; a rebranded platitude gets called out.

**The Named Challenge**
Skeleton: If you're [state], try the [Name] Challenge. + concrete rules immediately.
Evidence: 30 Day Buy Nothing Challenge (5.5x); Hard Decision Hack caption (5.2x). Naming appears in 4 of 8 educational winners.
Delivery: name it, rule it, then explain the deeper why; the name makes advice feel like a program.
Watch-out: rules must be doable this week; vague challenges don't get attempted or shared.

**The Contrarian Flip + Bridge**
Skeleton: By far the worst thing you can do right now is [praised common practice]. Let me explain.
Evidence: stop making (2023) value content (4.3x); date-stamping the old way frames habits as outdated without insulting anyone.
Delivery: "Let me explain" is the two-word bridge that buys a contrarian claim its runway; show old-vs-new immediately.
Watch-out: rubric rule applies, contrarian without a receipt is engagement bait.

**The Percentage Formula**
Skeleton: Want to [result]? [X]% [factor], [Y]% [factor], [Z]% [factor]...
Evidence: Crabby Patty Formula (2.1x, 1,578 comments on 42K views); live-updating table synced to speech.
Delivery: the surprising weight (5% editing) IS the shareable claim; total must hit 100.
Watch-out: weights are a stance, be ready to defend them in comments (that's the engagement).

**The Catch Twist**
Skeleton: Here are [number] [resources] to [result]. And here's the catch: [twist].
Evidence: 4 YouTube videos, none about social media (14.5x); the twist lands at second 4 as the first re-hook.
Delivery: the twist reframes the whole list and doubles as the curiosity engine.
Watch-out: the twist must be true of every item, or item 2 breaks the frame.

**The Analogy Walk-In** [delivery model]
Skeleton: Imagine for a second you [everyday scenario], and [absurd behavior happens].
Evidence: dante car dealership / awareness stages (0.9x). Graded low for a reason our rubric catches: the text hook and spoken hook never bridge. Use the analogy, fix the alignment.
Delivery: physical-world analogy first, then the marketing lesson it exposes, then credit the framework by name (Schwartz).
Watch-out: bridge the on-screen text and the spoken open in the first sentence.

## Conventions (apply to every verbal hook)

1. **First three words are their own mini-hook** and are graded that way (rubric, verbal checks). "Just a reminder," "Delete that video," "If you're actual."
2. **The caption is a separate hook slot.** Five confirmed splits in the dataset where the caption runs a different hook than the video's spoken open. Write the caption from the written/ goal section, deliberately, every time.
3. **Repetition is a legitimate device.** Verbatim command repeats (150.2x) and single-sentence delivery loops (6.0x) both verified.
4. **Catchphrases are cross-video retention.** A repeated signature line ("thank me later," "my friend") builds parasocial equity across a feed, not within one video.
5. **Write for the ear.** Contractions, spoken rhythm, no subordinate-clause openings. Read every hook aloud before grading it.
